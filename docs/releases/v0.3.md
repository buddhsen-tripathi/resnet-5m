Weights for both completed runs from rounds 8–9. Each file is a single ResNet trained from scratch on the permitted 50,000 CIFAR-10 training images, distilled from ten of our own teachers.

| `R8_base_300.pt` | R6 baseline recipe with a 300-epoch cosine schedule | 300 | 4,995,226 | 97.25% | 97.46% | 0.895 |
| `R9_combined.pt` | AugMix, 8px Cutout, softer distillation and supervised contrastive learning | 200 | 4,937,794 | **97.54%** | **97.70%** | **0.901** |

R9 is our best recorded model on both evaluation sets. Its two-layer contrastive head adds 45,824 parameters during training, for **4,983,618 total trainable parameters**. The head is omitted from this inference export. Both runs used one H200 and seed 1. R8 resumed after epoch 113 following a VM interruption.

R8's extra epochs improved flip-averaged CIFAR-10 accuracy over R6 but reduced its Kaggle score from 0.898 to 0.895. R9 improved the public score to 0.901, three additional correct predictions on the 1,000-image public split. These are single-seed experiments; R9 combines several changes and narrows the final stage from 232 to 228 channels, so individual effects are not isolated. Private leaderboard results are pending.

`evaluate.py` prints each model's flip-TTA accuracy and an ensemble result; the table above reports the individual models. Prediction also requires the competition test files in `data/kaggle/`.

The files contain the exact student weights and configurations used for their Kaggle submissions. They work with `evaluate.py`, `predict.py` and `train.py --teacher`. They omit optimizer state and cannot be resumed with `--resume`. Full resumable checkpoints remain backed up locally. `round8-9.json` records configurations, evaluation results and SHA-256 checksums for both exports.
